Mat vs. Reformer Pilates: What's the Difference?

Devon Okafor · February 3, 2026 · 5 min read

New students ask us this almost every week: should I start with Mat or Reformer? Both classes are built on the same Pilates principles — control, precision, breath — but the equipment changes the experience enough that it's worth understanding before you book.

Mat Pilates: your own body as resistance

Mat classes use your bodyweight, gravity, and small props like resistance bands or a light ball. There's no machine to learn, which makes it the more approachable starting point for most people — you can focus entirely on form and breath instead of also learning equipment.

Reformer: spring resistance on a moving carriage

The reformer is a bed-like machine with a sliding carriage attached to springs. Adjusting spring tension changes the resistance, so the same exercise can be made easier or dramatically harder — which is part of why Reformer classes scale well from beginner to advanced in the same room.

So which should you book first?

If you've never done Pilates before, we generally recommend starting with a Mat class or the Intro session — it's the fastest way to learn the core vocabulary (neutral spine, scooped abs, controlled breath) without also thinking about machine setup. Once those basics feel familiar, Reformer adds resistance and range of motion that's hard to replicate on the mat alone.

That said, plenty of our members do Reformer first and pick it up fine — every Reformer class includes a two-minute orientation for new students, and instructors adjust spring tension for you.

Can I do both?

Most regular members do. A common pattern is Mat once or twice a week for control and core work, plus Reformer once or twice a week for strength — which is exactly why our class packs and unlimited membership cover both formats at no extra cost.
